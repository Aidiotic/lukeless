30-second preview clips from the public iTunes catalogue. Unofficial fan project — all music belongs to its artists. A companion to lukebox.

How to play

You get one second of a song from the playlist. Name it, or skip — either way the clip grows.

  1. Clip lengths: 1s → 2s → 4s → 7s → 11s → 16s
  2. Six tries. A wrong guess or a skip unlocks the next length.
  3. Solving faster scores more: 100 / 80 / 60 / 45 / 30 / 20 points.
  4. Reveal first word costs 25 points and tags the win with *. Solo only — not offered in 1v1 or Insane.

Daily gives everyone the same song, changing at midnight. Endless is random, forever. 1v1 puts you against a friend over six songs — you both race the same clip, and the round goes to whoever needs fewer seconds.

Insane: clips start at a tenth of a second — 0.1s → 0.5s → 0.7s → 1s → 1.5s, five tries instead of six, worth more per try (200 / 150 / 110 / 80 / 50). Search for a title and its result expands into six nearly identical versions — the real catalog title and five convincing fakes. You have to pick the exact recording. A wrong title costs two steps, so missing at 0.1s jumps straight to 0.7s. It also leans hard on lofi cuts where the catalogue has them. 1v1 has its own Normal/Insane toggle in the lobby — whichever the host picks before opening the match is what both of you play, and it can't be changed once a round is live. Insane 1v1 lasts fourteen rounds instead of six.

Clips come from Apple's previews, so they usually start at the hook rather than the first note. Consider it a feature.

1v1 runs directly between the two browsers, with no server keeping score. That also means both players' browsers hold the answers — it is a game between people who are not trying to cheat.
